What happens

When you call

  1. You reach the desk. It answers at any hour and takes the details down properly: what broke, when, who is affected, how to reach you.
  2. Brian calls you back. First question: what is getting worse right now?
  3. Stop the damage. Lock the account, cut the access, roll back the bad change, put up a holding page. Containment comes before diagnosis.
  4. Find the cause. Logs, timeline, what changed. Evidence is preserved, in case your insurer or your lawyer needs it later.
  5. Fix it and write it up. What happened, what we changed, and what to do so it doesn't happen again. Short, in plain English.

Most of the fires we put out had a smoke alarm nobody installed.

The other half

Better: don't have the Sev 1

The cheapest emergency is the one that never pages anyone. Before the bad day, we set up:

  • Watching on the things that make you money, so you hear about an outage before your customers do.
  • Backups that have been restored at least once. A backup nobody has tested is a guess.
  • Account hygiene: two-factor sign-in everywhere, a short list of who has admin, no accounts left behind by people who left.
  • Email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) so nobody else can send mail as you.
  • A watch on your brand name for fake stores and look-alike domains. How that works.
  • A runbook: what to do, who to call, and where the passwords live, written down before you need it.
  • A safety check on every AI agent before it acts. We gave the robots a babysitter.
